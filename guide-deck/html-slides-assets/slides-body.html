B.Tech Project  ·  Mid-semester briefing  ·  20 minutes

SurakshaNet scores WhatsApp on this laptop. This semester we taught it Hinglish without moving that detector.

Assume no earlier context. Part one is the shipped Chrome product. Part two is the data work: why Devanagari labels are not enough, what failed, what we kept, and which numbers we will defend.

Aamod Jain and Pratham Garg. Guide: Geeta Yadav. Indian Institute of Technology Ropar.

Abusive messages arrive on WhatsApp Web. Uploading the chat to score them would already be the wrong system.

The product requirement is local: an incoming line is scored on this device, a banner may appear, and nothing is sent to a server. Automatic blocking is also out of scope. That privacy constraint is why this is a systems project first, and a language project second.

Term — on-device. Inference runs inside Chrome on the user’s laptop. The model file, the score, and the incident log never leave the machine.

The detector is a Chrome extension, so the model has to fit in the browser and may only read incoming text.

We ship as Manifest V3. If WhatsApp changes its page structure, the extension fails closed rather than guessing. The incident log is a local JSON file. That size limit is the reason a 471 MB Hindi model was never a shipping option, even when it scored better on Devanagari.

Term — Chrome MV3. Manifest V3 is the current Chrome extension platform. It caps what we can bundle and forbids a silent cloud round-trip at inference.

The shipped product is three steps: read the chat, score it here, paint a banner.

Call them A, B and C. Every later idea in this talk has to leave those three steps alone. Conversion of Hindi into Hinglish is training-time work. It is not a fourth box on this line, and it does not run when a message arrives.

XLM-R would score Hindi better, but 471 megabytes cannot ship inside Chrome.

The MACD paper ceiling on Hindi is XLM-R at 86.34%. We cannot carry that. We fine-tune multilingual MiniLM and quantize it to INT8. Hindi dropped by under 0.11 percentage points. Latency is 28 milliseconds. The detector is small enough. The missing language is the real problem.

Term — INT8. 8-bit integer quantization shrinks the ONNX weights so the model fits in the extension. FP32 is the full-precision checkpoint we train; INT8 is what Chrome loads.

The poster measured Hindi and English. It never measured how India actually types on WhatsApp.

Train set 42,487. Hindi 84.66%, mixed Hindi-plus-English 88.01%. Those bars are real. The striped bar is not a low score. It is a measurement we did not have. Filling it, without touching A, B or C, is the rest of this talk.

Labeled MACD is Devanagari. WhatsApp traffic is Roman Hinglish with Hindi grammar.

We have gold labels for the left screen. The product sees the right screen. If we train only on script, the model can look accurate in a paper and still miss ordinary Indian chat. The meeting line is not abuse. It is the register we were missing.

Term — Hinglish. Hindi as the matrix language, written in Roman letters, with English words mixed in. It is not ITRANS, and it is not Devanagari typed in another font.

We inherit two labeled corpora. We do not invent a new abuse ontology.

MACD is Hindi ShareChat-scale comments (Gupta et al., 2022). Davidson is English (2017). Class 0 is abusive, class 1 is not. That mapping is frozen for the whole project. On briefing slides we show class-1 text only. Class 0 is kept as a count, not as a quote.

Term — MACD. Multilingual Abusive Comment Detection. Hindi comments with published labels. There is no aligned English sentence sitting next to each row.

Research question

Can we rewrite MACD Hindi into conversational Hinglish, keep the published labels honest, and measure the detector on a test we freeze before training?

Three constraints ride with that sentence. The Chrome path does not change. Synthetic or cleaned rows may try to help train, but they may not touch the frozen test. If a rewrite sanitizes class 0, the label is false and the row cannot enter train.

One question, three non-negotiables

Same MiniLM in Chrome. Same labels. Frozen Hinglish test of 6,368 lines, locked before we look at errors.

WAC and PAC need a parallel English sentence. MACD does not have one, so those mixers cannot build this train set.

HinGE is a human Hinglish benchmark from IIT Gandhinagar. Its rule systems, WAC and PAC, align Hindi with an English pair and then mix. That is the right paper for generation quality later. It is the wrong tool for constructing MACD train rows, because we would have to invent the missing English side.

Terms — HinGE, WAC, PAC. HinGE: human-generated Hinglish, n ≈ 1,964. WAC: word-aligned code-mixing. PAC: phrase-aligned code-mixing. Both require bitext.

The cheap hypothesis was to transliterate Devanagari and call that Hinglish. It fails a mix-depth test.

indic_transliteration maps letters. It needs no GPU. If the output were conversational mix, its code-mixing index would rise and its word-error rate against ITRANS would stay low. What we got was still Hindi in a different script. So we replace that step. We do not add a fifth box at inference.

Terms — CMI and WER. CMI, code-mixing index (Das and Gambäck): 0 means one language, higher means a deeper mix. WER, word error rate: (substitutions + deletions + insertions) / reference length. Low WER versus ITRANS means “just transliteration.”

Transliteration changed the script. The language was still Hindi, so CMI stayed near zero.

kala mITiMga hai cAra baje is not how anyone chats. Every token is still Hindi. Script conversion is not the register in the research question. The target is the third column: English letters, Hindi grammar, English words inside the sentence.

The target register. English letters, Hindi grammar, English words mixed in.

We replaced transliteration with Qwen3-32B and took that step off the laptop.

A, B and C do not move. Qwen runs once, offline, on Bedrock Mantle in us-east-1. Hindi in, conversational Hinglish out. 31,663 lines were converted. Qwen is a converter, not a detector, and it is not shipped inside Chrome. If the detector had to call Qwen live, the privacy claim would already be false.

Qwen likes to sanitize abuse. Quarantine exists so a polite sentence cannot keep label 0.

A row is accepted only if it is Roman, non-empty, not a refusal, still fires the abuse lexicon when the source was class 0, and does not collapse the MiniLM abuse score by more than 0.15. Failures go to quarantine. They are not deleted from the record. They are held out of train. On the converted test split, 89.9% of abusive lines still fire. Yield is 94.7%.

Term — quarantine. A held-out file of rejected rewrites, with a reason code: score drop, missing lexicon stem, collapsed length, refusal, or leftover Devanagari. Train quarantine n = 1,263.

The Hinglish test of 6,368 lines was frozen before we trained. Later cleaning is not allowed to move it.

If we rewrote the test after looking at errors, 84.36% would no longer be comparable to the poster. Layer-1 cleaning and 977 synthetic rows were tried on train only. They are allowed to fail. They are not allowed onto this split. Rescued quarantine from val/test, if any, goes into train, never back into test.

Allowed, because it does not move the test Clean train. Quarantine spikes. Hold synth out of val/test. Report CMI even when it is low. Retrain MiniLM on gated Qwen.
Not allowed, because it would make 84.36% meaningless Rewrite the frozen 6,368 after looking at errors. Ship a nearby ablation because it looks more sophisticated. Put Qwen inside Chrome.

The first mix dropped Hindi MACD from train. Devanagari Hindi fell to 70.04%. That run is not the model.

Hinglish-only training taught the detector the new register and forgot the old one. The corrected protocol puts Hindi MACD back, keeps Davidson English at 80/10/10, and adds gated Hinglish. Train 58,929 / val 15,581 / test 15,575. Hindi recovered to 85.14%, which is above the poster 84.66%.

On HinGE, Hindi-only Qwen matches PAC on BLEU and loses to WAC on mix depth, which is expected without bitext.

We ran the same converter on HinGE Hindi (n = 1,964) and compared it with the published WAC and PAC outputs against human Hinglish. Qwen BLEU is 17.7, PAC is 17.9, WAC is 16.4. WAC still wins WER and CMI because it aligns English and Hindi. Our production converter is Hindi-only, like MACD. This table evaluates the converter. It does not replace the detector numbers.

On the frozen val split, the largest error bucket is not obfuscation. It is the source label looking wrong.

INT8 val Hinglish: 507 false positives, 528 missed abuse, n = 6,375. Cluster labels are a research reading of the errors, not a second gold. “Source label wrong” is the mode. That matters for the viva: some of the remaining 16% is MACD noise, and cleaning it on the test set is exactly the thing the freeze forbids.

We will defend on-device scoring, a frozen Hinglish test, and a converter that is not inside Chrome.

WhatsApp is scored on this laptop at 113 MB and 28 ms. Hinglish is 84.36% on 6,368 held-out lines. Hindi was recovered after a 70.04% failure. The converter is gated Qwen, not transliteration, and not untested synth. CMI 13.7 is reported. The open problem is mix depth toward human Hinglish, without sanitizing class 0 and without unfreezing the test.
